# The Veteran's Guide to Simulator Flooring: Hitting Mats, Putting Turf, and Subfloors

Nothing ruins a $10,000 simulator setup faster than a cheap piece of carpet masquerading as a putting green. Worse yet, nothing ruins a golfer's season faster than hitting thousands of balls off a concrete-backed budget mat. I've been there. I’ve blown out my lead wrist and spent three months rehabbing golfer's elbow because I thought I could save a few hundred bucks on the hitting surface. 

Simulator flooring is not an afterthought; it is the foundation of the entire experience. It dictates your physical health, the accuracy of your launch monitor, and the aesthetic cohesion of the room. Let’s break down the flooring ecosystem—the subfloor, the stance area, the hitting strip, and the putting turf—from the perspective of a guy who has torn it all up and started over more than once.

## 1. The Subfloor: You Can't Build on Sand

You cannot just roll out premium putting turf on a bare concrete garage floor or an uneven basement pad. The subfloor determines how the ball rolls and how your joints feel.

### 1.1 The Concrete Problem
Concrete is cold, damp (especially in Canada), and unforgiving. If you lay turf directly on concrete, every fat iron shot sends shockwaves up your shaft, and the ball roll will feel artificial and skiddy.

### 1.2 The EVA Foam Solution
For most setups, 1-inch thick interlocking EVA foam gym tiles are the best subfloor. 
- **The Benefits**: They provide acoustic dampening, insulate against cold floors, and give the putting turf a realistic "give" when you walk on it, simulating real soil. 
- **The Execution**: Tape the seams of the EVA tiles with heavy-duty duct tape before laying your turf. If you don't, the tiles will shift over time, creating seams and ridges that will deflect your putts.

### 1.3 The Plywood Deck (The Premium Route)
If you are building a permanent, high-end room and want to integrate putting cups, you need a wooden deck. 
- Frame it with 2x4s (16-inch on center) and top it with 3/4-inch OSB or plywood. 
- This allows you to cut standard 4-inch deep holes for regulation putting cups. It’s significantly more work, but the sound of the ball dropping into a real plastic cup in your basement is deeply satisfying.

## 2. The Hitting Strip: Protect Your Joints

This is the most critical 12x30 inch patch of real estate in your house. Do not skimp here. The physics of hitting off a mat are fundamentally different than taking a divot in real turf. On a mat, the club bounces; on grass, it digs.

### 2.1 The Danger of Dense Mats
Traditional dense-fiber driving range mats are designed for durability, not joint health. When you hit down on them, the club decelerates violently. Over a winter of simulator golf, this repetitive shock trauma will destroy your wrists and elbows.

### 2.2 The Forgiving Alternatives
You need a hitting strip designed to absorb the strike.
- **Fiberbuilt (The Bristle Approach)**: Fiberbuilt uses stiff nylon bristles. The club glides through them exactly like real grass, with zero shock. The downside? Some argue it masks fat shots too well (it’s almost impossible to hit a heavy shot on Fiberbuilt).
- **Holy Grail or EZ Tee Hybrid (The Divot Action)**: These are deep-pile turfs often mounted on a compressible foam or gel base. When you strike down, the turf literally compresses, simulating a divot. They offer incredible feedback (you *will* feel a fat shot) without the joint trauma. 
- **The Veteran Move**: Cut a void in your subfloor and drop in a premium 12x30 inch hitting strip. When it wears out in two years, you just pull it out and drop a new one in, rather than replacing a massive 5x5 stance mat.

## 3. The Stance Area: Stability and Geometry

Your stance area needs to be perfectly level with your hitting strip. If you are standing an inch higher or lower than the ball, it will ruin your swing mechanics over the winter.

- **Material**: The stance mat doesn't need to be premium hitting turf. It just needs to be stable and grip your shoes. A dense, low-pile turf glued to your EVA foam subfloor works perfectly.
- **Integration**: The key is seamless integration. The transition from the stance mat to the hitting strip to the putting turf should be flush. Any lips or ridges will catch your club on practice swings and look amateurish.

## 4. The Putting Turf: Speed and Stimp

Finally, the visual expanse of the room: the putting turf. If you are using GSPro or TGC19 and relying on the simulator software for putting, the physical turf is just for aesthetics and catching the ball. But if you actually want to practice putting offline, you need to understand stimp.

### 4.1 Stimp Readings Indoors
Most premium indoor putting turfs roll at a Stimp reading of 10 to 11 (medium-fast to fast).
- **Nylon vs. Poly**: Nylon turf is the gold standard for indoor putting. It resists crushing (footprints) far better than polypropylene, and the ball rolls truer without "grain" (the tendency of the ball to break toward the direction the fibers are leaning).
- **The "Brake" Issue**: Beware of very cheap turf. The ball will skid for the first two feet off the putter face before catching and rolling, and it will decelerate artificially fast. You want a turf where the ball rolls purely end-over-end immediately.

### 4.2 Installing the Turf
- **Tension is Everything**: Do not just unroll it and walk away. Turf needs to be stretched and anchored. If you have an EVA subfloor, use double-sided carpet tape extensively around the perimeter and under high-traffic areas. 
- **The Broom Trick**: Once installed, take a stiff push broom and aggressively brush the turf *against* the grain. This stands the fibers up, removes the memory of being rolled in a tube for shipping, and ensures a consistent roll.

## Conclusion

Your flooring system is the physical interface between you and the simulator. Spend the money on a premium hitting strip to save your joints, take the time to level your subfloor with EVA foam or plywood, and invest in high-quality nylon putting turf for a true roll. A well-executed floor not only looks like a million bucks but ensures you come out of the winter simulator season swinging freely, not nursing an injury.

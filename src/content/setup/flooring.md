# Golf Simulator Flooring Guide: Subfloors, Turf, and Layouts

The floor of your golf simulator is more than just something to stand on. It dictates the aesthetics of the room, protects the balls from bouncing erratically, manages moisture (crucial in Canadian basements and garages), and provides the foundation for your hitting mat. 

This guide covers everything you need to know about building the perfect golf simulator floor, with a focus on materials easily accessible in Canada.

## Layer 1: The Subfloor

Unless you are building in a finished room with existing carpet or hardwood, you are likely dealing with a bare concrete slab. You cannot simply roll out artificial turf on concrete. You need a subfloor for three reasons:
1. **Moisture Control:** Concrete sweats.
2. **Leveling:** Garages have sloped floors for drainage; you need a flat putting surface.
3. **Cushioning:** Balls bouncing off concrete covered in thin turf will ricochet dangerously.

### Option A: Dricore Panels (The Premium Canadian Choice)
Dricore panels (available at Home Depot Canada and Lowe's) are 2'x2' OSB wood panels with a raised plastic cleat system on the bottom. 
* **Pros:** Provides a perfect vapor barrier against cold concrete. Easy to install. Gives you a solid wood base to glue or staple premium turf onto.
* **Cons:** Adds about 7/8" to your floor height (which matters if ceilings are low). Costs about $2.00 - $2.50 CAD per square foot.

### Option B: Interlocking EVA Foam Tiles (The Budget DIY Choice)
Heavy-duty gym mats (1/2" to 1" thick) are the most popular DIY choice.
* **Pros:** Extremely cheap. Very soft, completely killing ball bounce. Easy to cut with a utility knife.
* **Cons:** Over time, heavy foot traffic can cause the foam to compress slightly. They expand and contract with temperature swings in unheated garages.
* **Sourcing:** Canadian Tire, Costco Canada, or Amazon.ca. Look for minimum 1/2" thickness.

## Layer 2: The Turf

Once your subfloor is down, you need to cover it. The type of turf you choose depends on whether you want to use the floor for putting practice.

### For Aesthetics and Bounce Reduction Only
If you just want the room to look nice and stop golf balls from bouncing wildly off the floor, you don't need expensive putting turf.
* **Solution:** Grizzly Grass or basic low-pile indoor/outdoor carpet.
* **Cost:** $1.00 - $1.50 CAD per sq/ft.
* **Where to buy:** Home Depot Canada sells large rolls off the rack. You can cover a 15x15 room for a few hundred dollars.

### For Realistic Putting
If you plan to putt out on your simulator (using GSPro or TGC 2019), you need premium putting turf that rolls true at a Stimp speed of 9 to 11.
* **Solution:** Premium Nylon Putting Turf.
* **Cost:** $4.00 - $7.00 CAD per sq/ft.
* **Sourcing in Canada:** SynLawn Canada, Rymar Grass (very popular in Canada), or Par2Pro. 
* **Installation:** Premium putting turf should be glued down to a rigid subfloor (like Dricore or plywood) using turf adhesive to prevent bubbling and ensure a perfectly smooth roll.

## Integrating the Hitting Mat

The cleanest, most professional-looking simulator floors integrate the hitting area directly into the floor so it is perfectly flush, rather than having a thick mat sitting on top of the turf.

### The "Drop-In" Method
1. Build your subfloor using 1-inch EVA foam.
2. Roll your putting turf over the entire room.
3. Mark out the dimensions of your hitting strip (e.g., 12" x 36" Fiberbuilt strip).
4. Use a sharp utility knife to cut through the turf and the EVA foam, removing the rectangle.
5. Drop your hitting strip into the hole. It should sit perfectly flush with the surrounding turf.

*Why this is crucial:* Standing on one level (the turf) while the ball sits 2 inches higher on a mat completely ruins your swing plane. You must either stand on a mat that is on the same level as the ball, or use a flush drop-in setup.

## Handling Garage Slopes

If you are building in a Canadian garage, the floor slopes toward the door for snowmelt drainage. This ruins putting on a simulator.
* **The Fix:** You must frame a level wooden subfloor using 2x4 sleepers. Find the high point of the garage, and use wood shims to level the 2x4s across the slope. Cover with 3/4" plywood or OSB. This is labor-intensive but mandatory for accurate putting and launch monitor leveling.

## Conclusion

Your flooring choice dictates the entire feel of the simulator room. For the best balance of budget and performance in Canada, use 1-inch EVA foam tiles covered in mid-grade Home Depot turf, and cut out a slot for a premium hitting strip. If budget is no object, lay a Dricore subfloor covered in Rymar putting turf for a true commercial aesthetic.

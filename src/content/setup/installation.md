## Overview: Installing a Home Golf Simulator in Canada

Installing an indoor golf simulator in a Canadian home requires balancing spatial dimensions, electrical requirements, and thermal management (especially in garages or unheated spaces). 

Whether building a DIY enclosure or commissioning a custom turnkey install, following a structured workflow ensures clean ball tracking, maximum safety, and protection for your property.

### Key Installation Steps

1. **Room Clearance Verification**: Confirm ceiling height is at least 9 feet. Measure full driver swing clearance with your tallest club.
2. **Electrical Setup**: Run dedicated wiring for high-lumen laser projectors, gaming PCs, and auxiliary turf heating.
3. **Enclosure Assembly**: Erect heavy-duty metal frame or modular enclosure with side safety nets and foam boundary padding.
4. **Impact Screen Mounting**: Tension premium 3-layer impact screen to minimize bounce-back while maintaining image clarity.
5. **Turf & Hitting Mat Placement**: Lay stance mat and shock-absorbing hitting strip flush with subfloor turf.
6. **Hardware & Tracking Calibration**: Mount launch monitor and overhead projector, calibrating field-of-view and target alignment in GSPro or FSX Play.

### Canadian Winter & Space Considerations

Canadian homes feature unique architectural traits. Basements in Ontario, Quebec, and Atlantic Canada usually provide solid concrete subfloors, but lower ductwork or ceiling joists can reduce effective clearance. Garage builds in Alberta and British Columbia require careful insulation and infrared heating to protect sensitive camera sensors during freezing temperatures.

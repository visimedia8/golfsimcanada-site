# Golf Simulator Shed Canada: Surviving the Winter

If your basement ceiling is too low and your garage is filled with cars and hockey gear, building a dedicated backyard golf simulator shed is the ultimate solution. However, building a structure in Canada that houses sensitive electronics and keeps you warm while swinging a golf club in January requires serious planning. 

This guide covers everything you need to know about building a four-season golf simulator shed in Canada, focusing on dimensions, insulation, heating, and realistic CAD costs.

## Optimal Dimensions for a Simulator Shed

When building from scratch, you have the luxury of dictating the dimensions. Do not skimp on space, as expanding a shed later is nearly impossible.

* **Minimum Recommended:** 12' Wide x 20' Deep x 10' High
* **Ideal Luxury Size:** 16' Wide x 24' Deep x 10' High (Vaulted or Flat Ceiling)

### Height is Critical
Aim for interior clear height of at least 10 feet. Remember that an exterior height of 10 feet will result in an interior height of around 9 feet once you factor in floor joists, subfloor, roof trusses, and drywall. Plan for 11-12 foot exterior walls if local bylaws permit.

### The "Over 108 Sq Ft" Rule in Canada
In almost all Canadian municipalities (Toronto, Calgary, Vancouver, etc.), any accessory building larger than 10 square meters (approx. 108 square feet) requires a building permit. A 12x20 shed is 240 square feet. **You will absolutely need a permit, engineered drawings, and inspections.** Do not try to fly under the radar with a building this large, as the municipality can force you to tear it down.

## Insulation: Defeating the Canadian Cold

A simulator shed is essentially a small house. If you want to use it when it's -25°C in Alberta or Ontario, insulation is the most critical phase of the build.

### 1. The Floor (Often Overlooked)
A cold floor makes the whole room feel freezing. 
* **Concrete Pad Build:** If building on a concrete slab, install rigid XPS foam insulation (R-10 to R-15) *under* the slab during pouring. Alternatively, build a raised subfloor inside with 2x4s and fill the cavities with rigid foam.
* **Wood Skid/Pier Build:** If your shed is raised on blocks or helical piles, you must insulate the floor joists heavily (R-30 batts) and seal the underside with a rodent-proof barrier.

### 2. The Walls
Use 2x6 framing instead of 2x4. The extra depth allows for R-22 fiberglass batts or Rockwool insulation. Rockwool is highly recommended as it is fire-resistant, water-resistant, and provides excellent soundproofing (so you don't annoy the neighbors with driver impacts).

### 3. The Ceiling
Heat rises. The ceiling needs the highest R-value in the shed.
* Aim for R-40 to R-50 in the attic space. 
* Spray foam (closed-cell) directly to the roof deck is highly effective in vaulted shed designs, providing both extreme insulation and a vapor barrier.

### Vapor Barrier
A continuous 6-mil poly vapor barrier on the warm side of the insulation is mandatory by Canadian building codes. Tape all seams perfectly. Simulator sheds get warm inside while being freezing outside, creating a massive condensation risk.

## Heating and Climate Control

You need a heat source that is safe, efficient, and capable of heating the space quickly.

### 1. Mini-Split Heat Pump (The Best Option)
A ductless mini-split (like a MrCool, Mitsubishi, or Senville) is the gold standard for Canadian sheds. 
* **Pros:** Provides powerful heating in winter and AC in the summer. Very energy-efficient. Extremely quiet.
* **Cons:** Higher upfront cost ($1,500 - $3,500 CAD installed).
* *Note for Canadians:* Ensure you buy a "Low Ambient" or "Hyper Heat" model rated to operate down to -25°C or -30°C. Standard heat pumps stop working around -10°C.

### 2. Electric Baseboard / Fan Forced Heaters
* **Pros:** Cheap to buy ($100-$300 CAD) and easy to install if you have a sub-panel in the shed.
* **Cons:** Expensive to run, take longer to heat the room, and take up wall space.

### 3. Radiant Ceiling Panels
* **Pros:** Keep the floor warm, save wall space, very comfortable heat.
* **Cons:** Expensive, requires careful placement so you don't hit them with your club.

*Never use unvented propane or kerosene heaters in a sealed, insulated simulator shed. The carbon monoxide risk is deadly, and they pump gallons of moisture into the air, which will destroy your electronics and projector.*

## Electrical Requirements

You must run an underground trench from your house's main panel to the shed.
* **Requirement:** A 60-Amp or 100-Amp sub-panel.
* **Needs:** Mini-split heater (requires 240V), lighting, gaming PC (draws 500W-800W), projector, launch monitor, and convenience outlets.
* **Cost:** Trenching and running armored Teck cable in Canada will typically cost $1,500 - $3,000 CAD depending on the distance from the house.

## Projector and Electronics in the Cold

Even in a well-insulated shed, if you turn the heat off for three days in February, the interior will drop below freezing.
* **LCD Projectors:** LCD panels can freeze and crack. DLP projectors are generally safer, but *all* projectors should be warmed up slowly. Do not blast a heater directly at a frozen projector and turn it on immediately. Keep the shed at a baseline 5°C-10°C when not in use.
* **Launch Monitors:** Batteries die quickly in the cold. Bring your iPad, Mevo+, or Garmin R10 inside the house when not in use. Permanent camera systems (Uneekor, GCHawk) will survive, but consult the manual for operating temps.

## Total Budget: What Does a Simulator Shed Cost in Canada? (CAD)

This assumes you are hiring contractors for the structure/electrical, but doing some finishing yourself.

### The Shed Structure
* Concrete Pad / Helical Piles: $2,500 - $5,000
* Framing, Siding, Roof (12x20x10): $10,000 - $18,000
* Insulation and Drywall: $3,000 - $5,000
* Electrical Sub-panel and Trenching: $2,000 - $3,500
* Heating (Mini-Split Installed): $2,500
* **Structure Subtotal: $20,000 - $34,000 CAD**

### The Simulator Equipment (Mid-Tier)
* Launch Monitor (SkyTrak+ / Bushnell Launch Pro): $4,000 - $5,000
* Enclosure & Screen (Carl's Place): $2,000
* Hitting Mat (Fiberbuilt): $1,200
* Projector & Mount: $1,500
* Gaming PC & Software: $2,000
* **Equipment Subtotal: $10,700 - $11,700 CAD**

**Total Estimated Cost: $30,700 - $45,700 CAD**

## Conclusion

Building a four-season golf simulator shed in Canada is a major investment, essentially adding a small addition to your home's square footage. By prioritizing insulation, a hyper-heat mini-split, and 10-foot ceilings, you will create an incredible winter sanctuary that adds significant value to your property and your golf game.

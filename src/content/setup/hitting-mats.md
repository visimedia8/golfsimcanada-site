# Golf Simulator Hitting Mats: A Guide for Canadians

Of all the components in a golf simulator build, the hitting mat is arguably the most important for your physical health. Hitting hundreds of balls off a cheap, thin mat placed directly over a Canadian basement concrete slab is a guaranteed recipe for golfer’s elbow, wrist injuries, and shoulder pain.

This guide will break down the mechanics of a good simulator mat, compare the top brands available to Canadians, and provide strategies for getting the best value in CAD.

## Why Cheap Mats Cause Injury

A real golf swing involves hitting down on the ball and taking a divot. The grass and dirt yield to the clubhead, absorbing the impact energy. 
Cheap simulator mats (often found on Amazon for under $100) are essentially just thin astroturf glued to a hard rubber backing. When your club strikes these mats, the energy has nowhere to go but straight back up the shaft into your hands and arms. Over time, this repetitive shock causes tendonitis. 

Furthermore, cheap mats cause "club bounce." If you hit the ball fat, the club bounces off the hard rubber and strikes the ball anyway, giving you a false sense of a good shot on the simulator.

## Top Hitting Mats for Joint Protection

To prevent injury and get realistic turf interaction, you need a premium mat. Here are the top choices.

### 1. Fiberbuilt (The Gold Standard for Joint Health)
Fiberbuilt mats are unique. Instead of traditional turf, they use a proprietary bristle technology. The hitting area is made of dense nylon fibers that physically give way when the club strikes, mimicking a real divot perfectly.
* **Pros:** Unmatched joint protection. Virtually indestructible. Extremely realistic turf interaction.
* **Cons:** Can be very "grabby" if you hit it extremely fat, which slows club speed down drastically. High cost.
* **Best Models:** Fiberbuilt Player Preferred Series or the traditional Studio Mat.
* **Canadian Availability:** Excellent. You can buy directly from Fiberbuilt (they have operations in Canada) or through Par2Pro in Alberta.
* **Cost (CAD):** $800 - $1,500+ for full mats. Hitting strips only: $250 - $400.

### 2. TrueStrike
TrueStrike mats use a unique silicone gel base underneath the hitting turf. When you strike the ball, the gel compresses, simulating the yield of natural turf.
* **Pros:** Excellent joint protection. Feels closer to real fairway grass than Fiberbuilt bristles. Forgiving on fat shots.
* **Cons:** The gel sections are heavy. The hitting turf eventually wears out and needs replacement.
* **Canadian Availability:** Available through Canadian distributors like Par2Pro and sometimes Golf Town.
* **Cost (CAD):** $1,000 - $1,600.

### 3. EZ Tee Hybrid / Holy Grail
These are newer entries into the market that use an incredibly dense, deep pile turf that absorbs impact without needing bristles or gel.
* **Pros:** Very realistic feel. Allows you to take a real tee and push it directly into the turf (no rubber tees needed). Cheaper than Fiberbuilt.
* **Cons:** Harder to source directly in Canada without paying US shipping/duties, though Canadian distributors are starting to carry them.
* **Cost (CAD):** Full mats $600 - $900. Strips $150 - $250.

## The DIY "Cut-In" Strategy (How to Save Money)

Dropping $1,500 CAD on a full 5x5 foot Fiberbuilt mat is tough to swallow for a budget build. The secret used by most DIY builders is the "Cut-In" method.

1. **Build a cheap stance area:** Buy heavy-duty interlocking EVA foam gym tiles (available at Canadian Tire or Costco). Cover them in cheap artificial turf from Home Depot.
2. **Buy just the hitting strip:** Buy a 12" x 36" premium hitting strip (like a Fiberbuilt or EZ Tee) for $200-$300.
3. **Drop it in:** Cut a hole in your cheap EVA foam floor that perfectly matches the dimensions of your premium hitting strip. Drop the strip in so it sits flush with your stance area.

*Result:* You get premium joint protection exactly where the club strikes the ground, but you save $1,000 on the area where you are just standing.

## Sourcing Mats in Canada

Shipping a 5x5 foot, 80-pound rubber mat from the USA to Canada via UPS/FedEx will easily cost you $200+ in shipping, plus another $100 in duties and brokerage fees. **You must source locally.**

* **Par2Pro (Edmonton, AB):** The best Canadian source for Fiberbuilt, TrueStrike, and raw turf materials.
* **Golf Town:** Keep an eye out online; they occasionally stock premium mats.
* **Costco Canada:** Costco online often sells Country Club Elite mats. While durable, be warned: Country Club Elite mats are known to be very tough on the joints for heavy swingers.

## Conclusion

Do not compromise on your hitting mat. The medical bills and physiotherapy costs for fixing golfer's elbow will far exceed the cost of a premium Fiberbuilt or TrueStrike mat. Use the cut-in strategy to save money, and ensure you are protecting your body for a long winter of indoor golf.

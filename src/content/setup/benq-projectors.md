# BenQ Golf Simulator Projectors: Canadian Setup Guide

Creating a truly immersive indoor golf experience requires more than just a good launch monitor and a sturdy screen; you need a projector that can throw a massive, bright, and crisp image from a short distance without casting shadows. In the world of golf simulators, **BenQ** has established itself as the dominant brand, specifically engineering projectors tailored to the unique demands of indoor golf.

For Canadian golfers setting up in basements, garages, or specialized sheds, understanding lumens, throw ratios, and laser vs. lamp technology is critical to avoiding a washed-out or awkwardly placed image. This guide breaks down everything you need to know about integrating BenQ projectors into your Canadian simulator build.

## Why BenQ Dominates the Simulator Market

Most off-the-shelf home theater projectors are designed to sit at the back of a dark room and throw an image across a long distance. Golf simulators require the exact opposite. 

1.  **Short Throw Lenses:** You need the projector mounted close to the screen (usually directly above or slightly in front of the golfer) so you don't cast a shadow on the screen during your swing. BenQ specializes in "Short Throw" (ST) projectors designed precisely for this geometry.
2.  **High Brightness:** Unlike a dark movie theater, you need ambient light in a golf simulator to safely swing a club and see the ball. BenQ simulator projectors push high lumen counts (typically 3,000 to 5,500 lumens) to cut through ambient room lighting, ensuring the digital golf course remains vibrant.
3.  **Golf Mode Color Calibration:** Many BenQ models feature an exclusive "Golf Mode." This color preset enhances greens and blues, making the digital grass, trees, and sky of software like GSPro or TGC 2019 pop with incredible realism.

## Laser vs. Lamp: Making the Choice

BenQ offers two distinct technologies: traditional lamp-based projectors and modern laser projectors. 

### Lamp-Based Projectors (e.g., BenQ TH671ST)
*   **The Technology:** Uses a traditional bulb to generate light.
*   **Pros:** Significantly cheaper upfront cost. Excellent entry point for budget simulators. The TH671ST is legendary for its value, offering 1080p resolution and low input lag.
*   **Cons:** Bulbs degrade over time. After 2,000 to 4,000 hours, the image will dim, and you will need to purchase a replacement bulb. They also take longer to warm up and cool down.
*   **Verdict:** Best for budget builds in spaces where you can tightly control the ambient light.

### Laser Projectors (e.g., BenQ LU935ST, LK936ST)
*   **The Technology:** Uses a laser diode light source. 
*   **Pros:** Incredible longevity (up to 20,000 hours of maintenance-free lifespan). Zero degradation in brightness over time. Instant on/off capabilities. Superior color accuracy and contrast.
*   **Cons:** High initial purchase price.
*   **Verdict:** The ultimate choice for high-end simulators. If you are building a premium setup and want a "set it and forget it" solution with jaw-dropping visuals, BenQ's laser lineup is unmatched.

## Placement and Aspect Ratios in Canadian Basements

Mounting the projector correctly is the trickiest part of a simulator build. You must calculate the Throw Distance (the distance from the lens to the screen) to ensure the image fills your impact screen perfectly.

*   **Aspect Ratios:** 
    *   *4:3 (Square-ish):* Common in low-ceiling Canadian basements (e.g., an 8ft tall by 10.5ft wide screen). You need a projector that supports 4:3 natively or via scaling.
    *   *16:9 / 16:10 (Widescreen):* Ideal for spaces with 9ft to 10ft ceilings, offering a cinematic, immersive view.
*   **Ceiling Mounts vs. Floor Enclosures:** While ceiling mounting is standard, some Canadian basements have exposed joists or low ductwork that makes ceiling mounting dangerous for the projector (errant swings). In these cases, consider a floor-mounted projector enclosure placed safely in front of the hitting area.

# Foresight GC3 vs GCQuad: The Hard Truth for Home Simulator Builders

Listen up. If you've been in the simulator game as long as I have, you know that chasing data can become an obsession. When you’re freezing in a Canadian garage in February, hitting off a mat that is slowly wrecking your left wrist (pro tip: invest in a Fiberbuilt or Carl's HotShot mat, trust me), the last thing you want is your launch monitor lying to you.

Foresight Sports is the gold standard for indoor photometric systems. Radar units like Trackman are great on the range, but indoors? Give me a camera system any day of the week. No messy algorithms guessing spin because your basement is two feet too short. 

But here is the million-dollar question: Do you drop ~$8,500 CAD on the **GC3**, or do you remortgage the house and spend $20,000+ CAD on the **GCQuad**? Let's cut through the marketing fluff and look at the real-world differences.

## 1. Three Cameras vs Four: What Does it Actually Mean?

The GC3 has three cameras; the GCQuad has four. But what does that extra camera actually do?

Both units capture ball data flawlessly. Ball speed, launch angle, total spin, spin axis—they are identical. If you are just looking to play GSPro with the boys on a Friday night, the ball flight on the screen will be exactly the same whether you paid 8 grand or 20 grand. 

The difference is **Club Data**.

### The GC3 Club Data
The GC3 gives you the basics: Clubhead Speed, Smash Factor, Angle of Attack, and Club Path. For 95% of golfers, this is plenty. It tells you if you are swinging out-to-in and how steep you are.

### The GCQuad Club Data
The Quad gives you all of the above, plus Face Angle, Dynamic Loft, Lie Angle, and exact Impact Location on the clubface. It does this via four reflective stickers you have to put on your clubface. 
*Veteran Note:* Putting stickers on your clubs is annoying. If you are just playing sim golf, you won't use them. If you are a scratch golfer grinding to fix a two-degree face-to-path discrepancy, you need them.

## 2. Setup and Usability 

Both units are a dream for tight Canadian setups. You put them down next to the ball, and you hit. No 10-feet-behind-the-ball nonsense. 

However, both suffer from the same fatal flaw for a mixed-dexterity group: **They sit on the side.** 
If your buddy is a lefty, you have to pick the unit up, move it to the other side of the mat, align it, and switch the software. It’s a massive pain in the ass. If you have lefties in your regular foursome, look at an overhead unit like the Uneekor EYE XO instead.

### Hitting Zone Size
The GCQuad has a significantly larger hitting zone (the area where the camera can "see" the ball). With the GC3, you have a much tighter window. It’s not a dealbreaker, but the Quad is definitely more forgiving if you lazily drag a ball over to hit.

## 3. The Price to Performance Ratio

Let's talk CAD. 
- **GC3:** ~$8,500 CAD.
- **GCQuad:** ~$19,000 CAD base, plus ~$4,000 for club data. 

Is the Quad three times better? Absolutely not. For a home simulator, the GC3 is the undisputed king of value. It gives you 99% of the simulation experience for a fraction of the cost. 

## 4. Putting: The Hidden Frustration

Simulator putting is always a bit janky, but Foresight does it best. The Quad is marginally better at picking up subtle putts, but honestly? Most veterans turn on auto-putt in GSPro anyway. Don't spend $20K just to two-putt every green on the simulator.

## Final Verdict

If you are a PGA Teaching Pro, a club fitter, or a guy with unlimited disposable income who demands absolute perfection, buy the **GCQuad**. It is the best machine ever built.

If you are a serious golfer trying to build a reliable, lag-free, highly accurate indoor simulator in your house, buy the **GC3**. Take the $12,000 you saved, buy a top-tier projector, a high-end gaming PC for GSPro, a premium hitting mat that won't destroy your joints, and maybe a kegerator. You'll thank me later.

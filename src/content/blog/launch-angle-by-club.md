# Optimal Launch Angle by Club: Real Numbers from the Sim Bay

Look, I’ve built three different simulator bays in my lifetime, from a makeshift garage setup with a sketchy net to a full-blown commercial-grade photometric bay. I’ve hit tens of thousands of balls off mats that range from concrete-hard wrist-breakers to premium divot-action turf. If there’s one thing I’ve learned chasing numbers on a screen, it’s that guessing your launch angle is a fool's errand. 

Whether you’re using a radar unit that needs 10 feet of flight to calculate or a photometric system that grabs the data at impact, understanding and optimizing your launch angle is non-negotiable if you want to stop leaving yards on the table. Let’s cut through the marketing fluff and look at the real science of trajectory.

## What is Launch Angle, Really?

In the sim world, launch angle is the vertical angle at which the golf ball takes off relative to the horizon immediately after impact. It’s dictated by your **dynamic loft** (what the clubface loft actually is when it smashes the ball) and your **angle of attack** (whether you're hitting up or chopping down on it).

Don't let the software fool you. If your screen is showing a 20-degree launch with a driver, you’re either scooping it, or you set the unit up unlevel. 

## The Driver: Chasing the High Launch, Low Spin Dragon

For the driver, we all know the mantra: "High Launch, Low Spin." But what does that actually mean when you're sweating in your garage trying to hit 300 yards on GSPro?

### Ideal Driver Launch Angles by Swing Speed

Your optimal driver launch depends entirely on how fast you swing the stick. If you swing 85 mph and try to launch it at 10 degrees like a Tour pro, it’s going to fall out of the sky like a dead duck.

*   **Swing Speed < 85 mph:** Optimal Launch: **14° - 16°**
*   **Swing Speed 85 - 95 mph:** Optimal Launch: **13° - 15°**
*   **Swing Speed 95 - 105 mph (Most of us here):** Optimal Launch: **12° - 14°**
*   **Swing Speed 105 - 115 mph:** Optimal Launch: **10° - 12°**
*   **Speed Freaks (> 115 mph):** Optimal Launch: **8° - 10°** 

### How to Fix Your Driver Launch in the Bay

If your launch monitor is spitting out garbage numbers, first make sure it's reading correctly. Indoor radar units (like the Mevo+ or older TrackMans) can struggle with driver spin and launch if you don't have enough distance from the tee to the screen. Assuming the read is good, you need to hit up on the ball. 

Tee it high. Play it forward. Tilt your spine away. You want a positive (+2° to +4°) angle of attack. If your sim shows a negative attack angle with a driver, you are leaving 20 yards on the table, period.

## Fairway Woods and Hybrids: Holding the Virtual Green

Fairway woods on a simulator mat can be brutal. If your mat is too thin, you’ll bounce the club into the ball and get a falsely high launch. You need a neutral or slightly descending blow (-1° to -3°). Let the loft do the work.

*   **3-Wood (15°):** 9° - 11°
*   **5-Wood (18°):** 11° - 13°
*   **Hybrids (19°-22°):** 12° - 14°

## The Irons: Don't Let the Mat Lie to You

Here’s where simulator players get into trouble. Hitting off a cheap, hard mat causes you to subconsciously shallow out your swing to avoid breaking your wrists. This leads to a scooping motion, adding dynamic loft and skyrocketing your launch angle.

PGA Tour players launch their irons much lower than you think because they trap the ball. 

*   **4-Iron:** 11° - 12°
*   **6-Iron:** 14° - 15°
*   **8-Iron:** 18° - 19°
*   **Pitching Wedge:** 22° - 24°

A good rule of thumb I use in my bay: your iron launch angle should be roughly half of its static loft. If you’re launching a 34-degree 7-iron at 22 degrees, you are flipping your hands. Get some forward shaft lean.

## The Wedges: Flighting it Down

Amateurs launch wedges high with no spin. Pros launch wedges low with massive spin. When you're playing a sim round and you have 60 yards in, you want to flight that lob wedge down to about 28°-30° with high friction. 

A word of warning: indoor spin decay is real, especially with radar. But if you hit a low, driving wedge, a good photometric unit like a GC3 or GCQuad will pick up that 9,000 RPM spin rate accurately.

## A Veteran's Advice

Don't just mindlessly beat balls into your impact screen. Use the launch monitor for what it was built for. Hit 10 shots with your 7-iron, check your average launch angle, and compare it to the benchmarks. If it's too high, work on your impact position. Trust the data, protect your wrists with a good mat, and keep grinding.

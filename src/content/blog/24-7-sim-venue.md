# The 24/7 Autonomous Golf Simulator Business: The Unvarnished Truth

Operating a 24/7 unstaffed facility sounds like a dream until a member snaps a driver and takes out your $2,000 impact screen at 3 AM with zero staff around to handle it. I’ve built these autonomous setups, and I’m telling you right now: the tech stack you choose is the only thing standing between a profitable passive income stream and an absolute operational nightmare. 

The days of paying an hourly manager to sit behind a desk and hand out rented clubs are dying, especially in the Canadian suburbs. The autonomous model is the future, but you have to build it like a fortress and run it like a software company.

## 1. Why 24/7 is the Future (The Economics)

In a traditional setup, labor eats your margins. You’re paying someone to be there on a Tuesday morning in July when the bays are empty. The 24/7 model flips the script. You build a premium environment, lock the door, and let the software handle the rest. 

You shift from hourly walk-in revenue to Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR). You sell memberships: 10 hours a month for $300, or unlimited off-peak for $250. When January hits in Edmonton and it's -30°C, your members are booking bays at 11 PM, and you aren't paying a dime in staffing.

## 2. The Holy Trinity of Autonomous Tech

If these three systems don't talk to each other flawlessly, your business will fail. Period.

### The Booking Engine
Don't try to build this yourself. Use CourtReserve or Skedda. Your members need an app on their phone where they can see bay availability, book a slot, and have their credit card charged automatically via Stripe. If the UI is clunky, you'll spend your days fielding angry text messages from boomers who can't figure out how to book a tee time.

### The Door (Access Control)
This is the most critical piece of hardware in the building. When John Smith books Bay 2 at 5:00 AM, the access control system (I highly recommend Kisi or Brivo) needs to generate a temporary digital key on his smartphone. The door unlocks at 4:50 AM and his credential expires when his time is up. No key fobs, no physical keys. If the system goes down, nobody gets in, so ensure you have a battery backup on the door strike and the network router.

### The Eye in the Sky (Security)
You are leaving hundreds of thousands of dollars of tech unattended. You need cloud-based CCTV (like Verkada). You need to be able to pull up the feed on your phone and see every corner of that facility in 4K. If someone brings in four unregistered guests and starts doing cartwheels on the putting turf, you need a two-way audio system to politely (or aggressively) tell them to pack up. 

## 3. Idiot-Proofing the Simulator Hardware

When there's no tech support in the building, the hardware must be indestructible and the software must be locked down.

### Ceiling-Mounted is Mandatory
Do not put a floor-mounted launch monitor in a 24/7 facility. Someone *will* accidentally kick a TrackMan 4 or hit a GCQuad with a shanked iron. It’s not a matter of if, it’s when. You must use ceiling-mounted systems like the **Uneekor EYE XO** or **Foresight GCHawk**. They are out of reach, they don't need to be moved for lefties, and they hold calibration.

### The Kiosk Lock-Down
Golfers are there to hit balls, not to browse YouTube or mess with Windows display settings. You must lock the simulator PC into Kiosk Mode. Use an industrial touch-screen monitor on a heavy steel podium. When the PC boots, it should launch directly into GSPro or E6 Connect, and users should have absolutely no access to the desktop. 

### Consumables That Survive
- **The Screen:** A cheap screen will tear in three weeks. Buy a Carl's Place Premium or an SQHQ multi-layer screen. Put heavy baffling behind it.
- **The Mat:** Use commercial-grade hitting strips that can be easily swapped out. I prefer Fiberbuilt for unstaffed setups because it takes a beating and prevents the wrist injuries that cause members to cancel their subscriptions.

## 4. The Canadian Liability Reality

You need a bulletproof liability waiver. It needs to be integrated into the booking software so a member cannot receive their first door credential until they have digitally signed it. You need to outline that they are responsible for their guests and liable for damages caused by negligent behavior (like throwing a club).

Insurance for a 24/7 facility in Canada is tricky. Underwriters will scrutinize your security protocols. Having documented CCTV, strict access logs, and environmental sensors (detecting water leaks or freezing pipes) will bring your premiums down from astronomical to manageable.

## The Bottom Line

Running an autonomous 24/7 sim venue isn't "passive" income—it's highly leveraged tech management. Build the physical bays like a tank, lock down the software ecosystem tight, and you'll have a facility that prints money while you sleep.

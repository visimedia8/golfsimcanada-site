# Launching a Mobile Golf Simulator Business: Lessons from the Road

Lugging a golf simulator across the Greater Toronto Area in the back of a Sprinter van in February will teach you very quickly what equipment actually survives the road and what is just marketing hype. The mobile simulator business—renting out setups for corporate events, trade shows, and weddings—is highly lucrative, but the logistics will eat you alive if you don't know what you're doing.

I've built permanent commercial bays, and I've built rigs designed to be torn down in 45 minutes. They are two completely different beasts. If you're looking to break into the mobile sim market in Canada, here is the unvarnished truth on how to build a rig that performs under pressure and actually makes a profit.

## 1. The Mobile Target Market

You aren't selling tee times to guys looking to grind on their swing path. You are selling an *attraction*. 

### Corporate Events and Trade Shows
This is where the real money is. A company renting a booth at a Vancouver trade show will happily pay $2,000 for a day rate to have a simulator in their booth. Why? Because it stops foot traffic cold. You can brand the inflatable enclosure with their logo, run long-drive contests, and capture emails for their sales team. 

### Private Events
Weddings and high-end bachelor parties. A simulator is a massive upgrade over a tired photo booth. It’s interactive, competitive, and keeps guests engaged.

## 2. The Mobile Tech Stack: Portability vs. Performance

You have to balance high-end realism with the fact that you need to carry this gear through a hotel loading dock.

### The Enclosure: Inflatable is King
Do not try to build a modular aluminum pipe-and-drape enclosure for every event unless you enjoy spending three hours assembling metal poles while the event planner yells at you. 
You need a commercial inflatable enclosure. Companies like Inflatable Design Group build heavy-duty setups that inflate with a blower in three minutes. They look massive, they contain errant shanks safely (crucial when drunk wedding guests are swinging), and they pack down into a duffel bag.

### The Launch Monitor: Camera over Radar (Usually)
For mobile events, space is always your enemy. 
- **Radar (TrackMan 4 / Mevo+):** Radar needs 8 feet behind the ball and 10+ feet of flight. It also hates interference. If you set up a TrackMan at a trade show with heavy WiFi interference and metal scaffolding everywhere, you will get dropped shots and misreads. It looks prestigious, but it can be a headache indoors.
- **Photometric / Camera (GC3 / Bushnell Launch Pro / SkyTrak+):** This is my go-to for mobile indoor events. They sit right next to the ball, requiring almost zero depth behind the golfer. They are immune to WiFi interference and metal structures. The GC3 is rugged, accurate, and sets up in seconds. 

### Computing and Display
Do not bring a desktop PC to a mobile event. A bumped power cord means a hard crash. You need a high-end gaming laptop (RTX 4070 or better) because it has a battery. If the venue power blips, your system stays alive.
For projection, you need a cannon. Trade shows and outdoor tents are bright. A 3,000-lumen home theater projector will look completely washed out. You need a 5,000+ lumen laser projector. It’s expensive, but a washed-out screen makes your whole setup look cheap.

## 3. The Logistics: Where Amateurs Fail

The difference between a profitable mobile business and a stressful nightmare is in the logistics.

### Power Management
Never assume the venue has the power you need. A high-end PC, a 5000-lumen projector, and an inflatable blower pull serious wattage. You need dedicated 15-amp or 20-amp circuits. If you share a circuit with the DJ or the catering chafing dishes, you will trip the breaker mid-event. For outdoor events in the summer, you need your own clean-power inverter generators (like a pair of Honda EU2200is run in parallel). 

### The Mat
You need a mat that is light enough to carry but heavy enough not to slide on a polished concrete floor when a 250lb guy takes an aggressive swing. I recommend modular turf systems that piece together like puzzle mats, allowing you to build a stable stance area that breaks down easily for transport.

## 4. Pricing Your Pain

Do not price this like an hourly bay rental at a commercial facility. You are providing a managed, high-end entertainment service.

- **Minimums:** Never leave the driveway for less than a 4-hour minimum block. The setup and teardown take the same amount of time whether they use it for one hour or six.
- **The Rate:** Depending on the market, $250 to $400 CAD per hour is standard. 
- **The Attendant:** You (or an employee) must remain with the simulator at all times. You are there to tee up balls for beginners, manage the software, run the contests, and ensure nobody takes a chunk out of your $4,000 launch monitor with a pitching wedge. 

## The Bottom Line

A mobile golf simulator business is a logistics company that happens to use golf tech. Invest heavily in the enclosure and a rugged launch monitor, obsess over your setup checklists, and price your services to reflect the premium experience you're delivering. Do it right, and the margins in the Canadian event space are fantastic.
